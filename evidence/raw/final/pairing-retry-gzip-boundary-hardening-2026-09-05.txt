Finding A: pending pairing recovery ownership
- Before repair, PairingCoordinator.processDue() returned no ownership signal.
  SyncWorker could wake before nextAttemptAt, find only a future-due pairing,
  return success, and leave the next attempt to a 30-minute periodic worker.
  That interval exceeds the pairing transcript lifetime.
- Before repair, Chrome could attempt a failed pairing ACK on every 2.5-second
  UI poll, but after the first ACK acceptance it never republished while
  waiting for completion. The bootstrap secret also survived beyond the point
  at which Android's response had been authenticated.
- Android now returns retry while any non-expired pending pairing remains and
  both one-time entry points use a 10-second exponential WorkManager backoff.
- An initial response with zero positive matching relay confirmations remains
  non-active in pending_response and is scheduled for bounded retry rather than
  deleting the transcript/key and forcing another scan.
- Chrome now persists response_validated without the bootstrap secret before
  ACK network work, enters completion-reading after any completed send attempt
  even without a positive relay OK, queries completion before retry, and
  publishes a fresh ACK at most once per 30 seconds until completion or expiry.
- Regression results: Chrome pairing/recovery subset 20/20 PASS; Android JVM
  ownership test PASS; Android future-due Room instrumentation PASS on both
  physical phones and in the final full API-26 run.
- Final review after the first checkpoint added the no-positive-OK Android

Finding B: one-member gzip boundary
- The documented wire contract allowed exactly one deterministic RFC-1952
  member, but the TypeScript, Kotlin, Rust, and Swift convenience decoders all
  accepted a valid first member followed by another member or trailing data.
- Before the decoder repair, newly added concatenated-member assertions failed
  independently in Chrome, Android JVM, Rust, and Swift.
- TypeScript, Kotlin, and Swift now inflate one raw DEFLATE stream under the
  expanded-size ceiling, retain its exact consumed-input boundary, verify
  CRC32/ISIZE, and require the trailer to end the input. Rust now requires its
  one-member decoder cursor to consume the exact input.
- After repair: Chrome focused codec 10/10 PASS; Android ReaderGzip JVM PASS;
  Rust 6/6 plus 0 doc tests PASS; Swift 7/7 PASS. The real Android runtime
  rejects both concatenated members and a one-byte suffix on TCL, S23, and
  API-26.
